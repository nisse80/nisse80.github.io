Photo tips

  • Hold the iPhone straight in front of the display and let it fill most of the frame.
  • Avoid reflections from lamps and windows; tilt the monitor slightly if the display glares.
  • Numbers underlined in amber are ones Noteling is unsure about. Tap a number to correct it before saving.
  • You can always type a reading in by hand.
  • Importing older photos: Noteling uses the date each photo was taken. Rows marked Check need a quick look before saving.

Morning and evening

Readings taken before 15:00 count as morning, later ones as evening. You can change the date and time on the confirm screen before saving.

Apple Health

Noteling can save your own readings to Apple Health (it never reads your Health data). Turn this on or off in Noteling → Settings, or in the Health app → your profile → Apps → Noteling.

Subscriptions

  • Noteling is free for 5 photo readings and unlimited readings typed in by hand. Noteling Pro is available weekly or yearly; new subscribers get a 7-day free trial on the yearly plan.
  • Restore a purchase: Noteling → Settings → Restore purchases (or tap Restore purchases on the Noteling Pro screen).
  • Cancel anytime: iPhone Settings → your name → Subscriptions → Noteling, at least 24 hours before the period ends. The weekly plan renews every week until you cancel.
  • If you live in the EU, your statutory right of withdrawal is not affected; Apple handles withdrawals and refunds.
  • Refunds are handled by Apple: reportaproblem.apple.com.

Reference ranges

Noteling does not sort your readings into categories. For information, Settings → Help → Reference ranges shows the adult ranges published in the 2017 ACC/AHA guideline. Only your doctor can assess your values.

Your data

Your readings stay on your iPhone (and in Apple Health if you allow it). Noteling has no servers and no account. Details in the privacy policy.

Noteling is a personal diary for storing, organising and sharing blood pressure values you have already measured with your own monitor. It does not measure, diagnose or monitor any condition, does not recommend treatment and is not a medical device. If you feel unwell, contact your doctor or emergency services.
